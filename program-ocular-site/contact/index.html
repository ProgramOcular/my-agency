    Before you reach out

    How long does a project take?

    Most brand identity projects take 7 to 9 weeks from kickoff to launch: four weeks to diagnose, a week to define, then 2 to 4 weeks of design. Websites and larger projects usually take 10 to 14 weeks, depending on scope.

    Who will I be working with?

    With us, directly. There are no account managers in between: the people you meet on the first call are the people who design and build your project.

    How does payment work?

    We usually split it 50/50: a 50% deposit to start the project and the remaining 50% on delivery. Vision projects can be split into three milestone payments.

    Do you offer support after launch?

    Yes. Monthly maintenance is $500 a month and covers hosting checks, content updates and security patches, so your site keeps running smoothly.

    Can you work with our in-house team?

    Yes, and it often works well. We can lead the design or slot in alongside your marketing team, and every project ends with templates and guidelines your team can keep using.

    Do you work outside Ottawa?

    Yes. We work remotely with clients anywhere, in English and French.

    Brand guidelines

    The brand book.

    How we look, sound and show up. Use these rules for anything that carries our name, from a slide to a storefront.

    01Essence 02Logo 03Construction 04Size and misuse 05Colour 06Typography 07Graphic language 08Imagery 09Voice 10Components
    01 Essence

    We make brands visible.

    Ocular means “of the eye.” Everything we make is judged by one test: will people notice it, and will they remember it? Our mark is an aperture. Two interlocking brackets close around a single square of focus, the way a lens frames a subject. That idea runs through the whole system: frame the essential, cut the rest.

    Mission

    Give ambitious companies a brand people can't unsee.

    Promise

    Clear thinking, sharp craft and work that performs.

    Tagline

    Built to be seen.

    02 Logo

    One mark, three lockups.

    The horizontal lockup is the default. Use the mark alone where the name already appears nearby or space is tight, such as avatars, favicons and merchandise.

    03 Construction

    Built on a 14-unit grid.

    Every edge of the mark lands on a 14 × 14 grid. Corners are cut at exactly 45°. The aperture is a perfect 6 × 6 square, centred. Never redraw the mark; always use the master files.

    Grid14 × 14 units
    Corner chamfer2 units at 45°
    Aperture6 × 6 units, centred
    Notch3 units deep at 45°
    Symmetry180° rotational
    Mark to wordmark gap4 units
    WordmarkGeist SemiBold, −3% tracking
    04 Size and misuse

    Give it room. Keep it whole.

    Keep clear space equal to the aperture inset (4 units) on every side. The lockup should never appear smaller than 96 px wide on screen or 24 mm in print. The mark alone holds down to 16 px.

    05 Colour

    Mostly monochrome. One signal.

    Ink and Paper do the work. Signal orange is our accent colour: use it for a single point of focus per layout, like a link, a key number or the mark itself. Click a swatch to copy its hex value.

    07 Graphic language

    Borrowed from the mark.

    Three devices come straight from the logo. Use them with restraint; one per composition is usually enough.

    45° chamfer

    Cards and image frames get cut corners. Buttons stay simple pills so they read as controls, not content.

    The viewfinder

    Four corner brackets frame a subject. Use on hover states, in motion and to highlight one thing.

    Point of focus

    The aperture square

    A small square in Signal orange marks the active item, a bullet or the key point on a page.

    08 Imagery

    One subject. Tight frame.

    Photography should feel observed, not staged. Frame a single subject, crop with intent, use natural light and let shadows go deep. Colour comes from the subject, never from filters.

    09 Voice

    Say it like a sharp friend.

    We write the way we design: clear, confident and without filler. Short sentences. Concrete words. Every line should earn its place.

    Precise

    Specific numbers, real dates, plain words.

    Candid

    We say what we think, kindly and early.

    Composed

    Confident, never loud. No exclamation marks.

    Curious

    We ask better questions before giving answers.

    We say

    “You'll have the first direction on Friday.”

    We don't say

    “We're so excited to embark on this creative journey together!”

    We say

    “Sign-ups rose 41% in the first month.”

    We don't say

    “Our holistic, synergistic approach drives results.”
